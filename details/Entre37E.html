Pointe Laignel - Trywork Point

Born in Le Havre in 1769, Patrice-Gaspard Laignel began his seafaring career at a very young age. After serving in the merchant navy, he joined the French Royal Navy as a volunteer in 1786. During the stopover in Tenerife on 15 October 1791, shortly after the start of d’Entrecasteaux’s voyage, he was promoted to ensign.

Laignel was also firmly committed to the republican cause. During the early years of the French Revolution, he had belonged to the Friends of the Constitution club in Brest, and within the expedition he became close to officers and scientists who shared his convictions. This political dimension grew increasingly important after d’Entrecasteaux’s death in July 1793. His successor, d’Auribeau, declared himself a royalist, deepening the divisions already present among the expedition’s members.

These tensions became particularly apparent in the Dutch East Indies. Several republican members of the expedition, including Laignel, were detained in Java. He eventually reached Île de France in July 1794, where he rejoined several of his companions. He was subsequently given command of La Minerve and left Île de France for Europe on 21 October 1795, with Labillardière among those on board.

Laignel went on to have a long career in the French Navy, but it is above all his experience during the expedition that shows how closely exploration and hydrographic work were intertwined with the political upheavals of the Revolution.
