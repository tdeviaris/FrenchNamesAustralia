Sterile Island - Îles Stériles (the Actaeon Island Group)

For the expedition's officers, the Îles Stériles and the surrounding reefs were first and foremost navigational hazards whose positions had to be determined as accurately as possible. In his report on the reconnaissance of the coast near Recherche Bay, Lieutenant Alexis-Ignace de Crétin described his survey of 30 April 1792:

“I had received orders to examine the coasts lying east and north-east of Port du Nord. I left Recherche Bay on the morning of 30 April and was soon joined by the boat from the Espérance, commanded by M. de La Grandière, lieutenant de vaisseau. On leaving Port du Nord, we followed the coast on our port side. After running along the shore for about a league, we found ourselves among rocks extending far out to sea; some were level with the surface of the water, while others were relatively high… We followed the coast northwards in order to pass to windward of the Îles Stériles, leaving the dangers to starboard.”

This reconnaissance illustrates how closely the charting of the area was connected with navigational safety. It was necessary to determine not only the positions of the visible islands, but also those of the rocks, reefs and breakers extending offshore.

D'Entrecasteaux likewise emphasised this difficulty when describing the expedition's departure from Recherche Bay in May 1792. He explained that he had decided against attempting a passage between Anse des Ormiers and the Îles Stériles because it was not yet sufficiently well known:

“I should have liked to pass between Anse des Ormiers and the Îles Stériles; but at that time we did not know the passage well enough to attempt it. We therefore sailed very close to the breakers lying south of the Îles Stériles, and their position is consequently well established. I do not hesitate to regard the chart of this region as one of the most accurate produced during voyages undertaken in recent times” (May 1792, vol. 1, p. 79).

Beyond the caution imposed by these hazards, this passage also highlights the importance d'Entrecasteaux attached to the hydrographic work carried out in the region. Establishing the positions of reefs and breakers was one of the principal objectives of the surveys carried out from the expedition's boats and of the charting undertaken by Beautemps-Beaupré.
