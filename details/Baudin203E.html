Cap Sané - Cape Willoughby

The Friedland was an 80-gun ship of the Bucentaure class, built at Antwerp by the naval constructor Pierre Lair from designs by Jacques-Noël Sané. With two full gun decks, this type of vessel combined greater firepower than a 74-gun ship with widely recognised sailing and manoeuvring qualities. It represented one of the principal achievements of French wooden warship design under the First Empire.

The vessel was launched on 2 May 1810 in the presence of Napoleon, Marie-Louise, the King and Queen of Westphalia - Jérôme Bonaparte and Catherine of Württemberg - as well as the Duke of Bassano, Hugues-Bernard Maret, and the Duke of Istria, Jean-Baptiste Bessières. The sovereigns and their entourage were received at the arsenal by the Minister of the Navy, Decrès, Admiral Missiessy and the maritime prefect Malouet, in a pavilion erected to the right of the launching slip and shown in red in the painting.

After the Archbishop of Mechelen had blessed the vessel, the restraints holding it on the slipway were cut. The Friedland, launched stern-first, in accordance with contemporary practice, entered the water amid acclamations, music and artillery salutes. The operation was directed by Pierre Lair and Jacques-Noël Sané.
