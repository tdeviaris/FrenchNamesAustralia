Cap Trobriand - Cape Queen Elizabeth

The final stage of the expedition placed Trobriand in very different circumstances. After d’Entrecasteaux’s death and the arrival of the ships at Surabaya in October 1793, d’Auribeau sent him ashore in one of the Espérance’s boats to seek permission from the Dutch authorities for the expedition to land. France and the Dutch Republic, however, were by then at war, a development of which the expedition’s members were still unaware. Trobriand and the men in his boat were consequently detained as prisoners for several days.

The situation was resolved when the Governor-General of the Dutch possessions eventually decided that the safe-conducts issued to the expedition before its departure from Europe should be honoured. Trobriand was then able to return to the ships and bring this decision to d’Auribeau.

The political divisions within the expedition deepened in Java. Trobriand sided with the officers who remained loyal to the monarchy. When members of the expedition left Batavia, he was entrusted with part of the expedition’s papers and documents and embarked with a Dutch convoy.

A letter sent by the Duc d’Harcourt to Lord Grenville on 19 November 1795 makes it possible to follow the next stage of his journey. It states that Trobriand was travelling separately from Rossel and that he was intercepted by the HMS Sceptre before he had completed his business ashore. The expedition papers in his possession were seized by the British. Rossel, who later reached England, then made representations to the Admiralty in an effort to recover and preserve the maps, plans, drawings and journals brought back from the expedition.

Trobriand eventually returned to France. He continued his naval career, was promoted to capitaine de frégate, and later received command of La Vestale. He died at Le Cap, Saint-Domingue, in March 1799, in circumstances that remain unclear.
