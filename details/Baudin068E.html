Pauline Bonaparte (1780-1825), Napoleon’s younger sister, played a role in the dynastic and diplomatic politics of the imperial regime. In 1803, with her consent, Napoleon arranged her second marriage to the Roman prince Camillo Borghese in order to strengthen France’s ties with one of the great Italian aristocratic families.

This alliance helped install Pauline within the most prestigious circles of the Italian peninsula. Camillo Borghese commissioned the sculptor Antonio Canova to create a celebrated portrait of his wife, represented as Venus, the ancient goddess of beauty. In this sculpture, Pauline reclines while holding the apple offered by Venus to Paris in the Judgment of Paris. Executed between 1804 and 1809, the work became one of the most famous images of Pauline Bonaparte and of the Empire’s neoclassical aesthetic.
