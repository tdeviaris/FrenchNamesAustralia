Cap Sonnerat - Cape Sonnerat

Sonnerat was an important reference for the travelling naturalists of the early nineteenth century. His publications did more than catalogue species: plants and animals were frequently described in relation to the environments in which they occurred, while his narratives also paid close attention to the practices and technical knowledge of the peoples he encountered. In the Philippines, for example, he gave detailed descriptions of fishing devices, watercraft and their operation. This concern with natural environments, material objects and local technologies has clear parallels with the later work of François Péron and Charles-Alexandre Lesueur, who likewise documented watercraft, fishing implements and aspects of the material culture of the inhabitants of New Holland and Van Diemen’s Land.

Sonnerat was also part of an extensive network of scientific correspondence. He was a correspondent of the botanist Michel Adanson, exchanged specimens and observations with Jussieu, and maintained relations with Joseph Banks. Such exchanges illustrate the international circulation of natural-history knowledge at the end of the eighteenth century, even across the political divisions separating France and Britain.

His name also survives in botanical nomenclature. The genus Sonneratia, established by Carl Linnaeus the Younger in 1782, was named in his honour. It now includes several characteristic trees of tropical mangrove environments extending from East Africa through Asia and New Guinea to northern Australia. Sonnerat himself had observed and illustrated in New Guinea a plant belonging to this group, known locally as pagapate.

During his long years in India, Sonnerat continued his investigations and prepared a Nouveau voyage aux Indes orientales, completed in 1813. Unpublished at his death and long believed lost, the manuscript was eventually rediscovered in the Mitchell Library in Sydney and published in 2010. The work reflects the gradual expansion of natural history towards fields that would now be described as physical and human geography, including landforms, climate, soils, hydrography, resources and the relationships between societies and their environments.
