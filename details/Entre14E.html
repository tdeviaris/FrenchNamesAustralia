Île Lahaye - Hope Island

The name Île La Haye recalls the important place occupied by gardeners on major scientific expeditions, where their role extended far beyond caring for plants carried on board. Félix Lahaye, born at Caumont in the Eure in 1767, began working at a very young age in the botanical garden of the Académie des sciences in Rouen. In 1788, he joined the École botanique of the Jardin du Roi in Paris, where he worked under the direction of the botanist André Thouin. Three years later, he was selected as gardener for d'Entrecasteaux's expedition and, for his botanical work, placed under the direction of Labillardière.

Gardeners who took part in scientific voyages often possessed considerable botanical expertise. Lahaye assisted Labillardière in his work, but also formed his own collections and kept very detailed horticultural notes. During the expedition's landfalls, his duties included collecting seeds and plants as well as attempting to keep living specimens alive during long sea passages.

In Van Diemen's Land, he notably sowed some of the seeds brought from France. Later, in Tonga, he selected breadfruit plants for transport. He placed them in specially designed wooden cases fitted with drainage holes and frames that allowed them to be protected or ventilated according to the temperature. Despite the difficulties of the voyage, several of these plants survived as far as Java and were eventually transported to the Île-de-France, now Mauritius.

His collecting work was considerable. Under Labillardière's direction, he assembled 2,699 dried and numbered plant specimens during the expedition's various landfalls. Following the upheavals that came after d'Entrecasteaux's death and the detention of part of the expedition by the Dutch in Java, Lahaye continued to care for the breadfruit plants. He did not return to France until 1797.

His career then continued in some of France's major gardens: he became head gardener at Trianon in 1798, and later, in 1805, at Malmaison, the estate of Empress Joséphine. His herbarium, catalogue and journal were acquired in 1879 by the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle.
