Baie Marion - Marion Bay

Marc-Joseph Marion du Fresne (1724-1772) belonged to a generation of French navigators whose careers combined service in the Royal Navy, maritime trade and exploration. Based in the Île de France, now Mauritius, where he was also active as a planter and merchant, he had served as an officier bleu in the French Royal Navy and as a captain for the French East India Company.

At the invitation of Pierre Poivre, administrator of the Île de France, he left the island on 18 October 1771 in command of the Mascarin and the Marquis de Castries. One of the expedition’s original aims was to return Aotourou to Tahiti. Aotourou had travelled to France with Bougainville and was being sent back to the Pacific, but he died of smallpox in Madagascar on 6 November 1771. His death brought that part of the mission to an end, although Marion du Fresne continued the voyage of exploration into the southern seas.

After the two ships collided and suffered considerable damage, Marion du Fresne decided to make for the eastern coast of Van Diemen’s Land in order to carry out repairs, including replacing a mast, and to replenish the ships’ fresh-water supplies. The French reached the coast in early March 1772 and anchored in the bay now known as Marion Bay.

The stay was marked by an encounter with Aboriginal Tasmanians that escalated into violence. The account of Julien Crozet, Marion’s second-in-command, stresses the role of misunderstanding in the confrontation: after an initial encounter with two unarmed French sailors, the arrival of additional boats appears to have increased the inhabitants’ distrust, and they attempted to prevent the French from landing by throwing stones and weapons. The French then opened fire, and at least one Aboriginal man was killed.

After leaving Van Diemen’s Land, the expedition continued to New Zealand. In June 1772, after spending several weeks in the Bay of Islands, Marion du Fresne and twenty-four of his men were killed during a series of clashes with Māori. The precise causes of the violence remain debated; the surviving French subsequently carried out severe reprisals before leaving the region.
