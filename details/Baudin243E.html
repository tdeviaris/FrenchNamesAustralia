Île Percy - Calca Peninsula

Percy and François Péron both served with French armies operating along the Rhine during the Revolutionary Wars. Péron, who had enlisted in 1792, was taken prisoner in the spring of 1794 while, according to a biographical account, attempting to rescue a comrade trapped beneath the body of a horse. After being held in Germany and subsequently repatriated to France, he was discharged from the army following the loss of sight in his right eye and later began studying medicine. There is, however, no evidence that Percy and Péron ever met.

In 1799, Percy proposed a vehicle designed to carry a surgical team and its equipment rapidly towards the battlefield. Inspired by Bavarian artillery wagons and known as the wurst, this horse-drawn vehicle was pulled by six horses. The surgeons and their assistants sat astride a long central chest containing instruments, dressings and other supplies required for first aid.

Unlike Larrey’s flying ambulances (see Cape Larrey), which were primarily designed to collect and evacuate wounded soldiers, the wurst served mainly to transport surgical personnel and their equipment to places where medical assistance was needed. It was therefore intended to reduce the interval between injury and medical treatment.

The vehicle owed its name to its elongated shape, which was likened to a sausage, Wurst in German. Unwieldy and unable to evacuate wounded soldiers itself, it saw only limited use. It nevertheless illustrates Percy’s efforts to organise a mobile surgical service and bring medical assistance closer to the battlefield.
