Îlot des Dauphins - Rocky Island

No known account of the expedition indicates that the dolphins around Îlot des Dauphins were subjected to detailed zoological observation. Péron did, however, study several cetaceans during the voyage, particularly in Tasmanian waters. On 11 January 1802, he described a blue-black and white dolphin as a new species under the name Delphinus leucoramphus. Lacépède subsequently named it Delphinus peronii in his honour. The species is now known as the southern right whale dolphin, Lissodelphis peronii.

Péron connected this observation with a broader reflection on the coloration of animals inhabiting high latitudes:

“On the 11th [of January 1802], I described, under the name of the white-snouted dolphin (Delphinus leucoramphus), a new species of dolphin which M. de Lacépède, in his Histoire des Cétacées, was kind enough to name after me (Delphinus peronii). It is, moreover, a most remarkable phenomenon that all animals appear increasingly disposed to become white as their habitat approaches the high latitudes. Thus, in the same northern regions that produce white foxes, white bears, and so forth, there is also a species of white dolphin (Delphinus leucas, Linnaeus); and in the high southern latitudes, in addition to the Delphinus leucoramphus of which I have just spoken, there is a second species of the same genus, first observed by Commerson and subsequently by Forster, whose entirely white body retains only bluish-brown markings.”

Zoological terminology has since been extensively revised. Delphinus peronii is now classified as Lissodelphis peronii. This oceanic species of cool temperate and subantarctic waters should not be confused with the bottlenose dolphins inhabiting the Coffin Bay region. The populations studied in the inner waters of the bay have been identified by researchers as southern Australian bottlenose dolphins, provisionally designated Tursiops cf. australis. In the absence of a precise zoological account from the expedition, however, it is not possible to determine with certainty which species inspired the name Îlot des Dauphins.
