D’Entrecasteaux reports (translated from the French): “Our enforced stay at this anchorage enabled us to check the daily rate of chronometer no. 14. It gained 6.59 seconds in twenty-four hours relative to mean time. On 16 December, at 5 h 7 min, it was 8 h 0 min 21.55 sec behind the mean time at Esperance Bay. On the 16th, at 8:31 in the evening, the occultation of the star β Capricorni was observed aboard La Recherche. The result of the calculation, referred to Île de l’Observatoire, gave us the island’s longitude with great precision, placing it at 119° 34′ 35″ east. The longitude obtained from lunar distances from the sun observed at sea, and referred to Île de l’Observatoire, is 119° 27′ 14″, that is, 7′ 21″ less than the preceding value. The latitude of Île de l’Observatoire is 33° 55′ 17″ south…” Bruni-d'Entrecasteaux, J.-A., and e. Mr-de-Rossel (1808), Voyage de Dentrecasteaux envoyé à la recherche de La Pérouse, Volume 1, Imprimerie Impériale, pp. 210–211.
