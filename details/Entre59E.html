Ile de l’Observatoire - Observatory Island

Île de l’Observatoire served as an important geographical reference point for the expedition. Astronomical observations made there helped to establish the position of the anchorage more precisely and to check the accuracy of navigational instruments. The island also provided a collecting ground for the expedition’s naturalists.

The naturalist Jacques-Julien Houtou de Labillardière landed there on several occasions and collected plants then unknown to European science. Among them was what he described as “a new and very remarkable species of Eucalyptus”, which he named Eucalyptus cornuta after the distinctive form of its calyx. At the foot of the higher ground, “in marly soil”, he also collected a leguminous plant that he regarded as belonging to a new genus:

“It forms a new genus which I call Chorizema.”

The distinctive form of its leaves led him to name the species Chorizema ilicifolia. His observations, accompanied by detailed morphological descriptions, were subsequently published and illustrated in the Relation du voyage à la recherche de La Pérouse and its atlas. They illustrate how the geographical reconnaissance of an unfamiliar coastline was accompanied by the systematic collection and classification of its natural history.
