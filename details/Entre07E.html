Southport Lagoon - Baie des Moules

Lieutenant Saint-Aignan and the hydrographer Beautemps-Beaupré noticed that medium-sized vessels could seek shelter from any kind of wind in the Baie des Moules if they anchored behind two islets or rocks, now known as the Pelican Islands in Deephole Bay, in water four or five fathoms deep and with a sandy bottom. They also “found a great quantity of mussels and other shellfish on the bank of a kind of river or canal with salty water.” They were probably referring to the narrow opening on the western side of Baie des Moules leading to Hastings Bay, unless they saw the shells along the narrow entrance to a side arm of Baie des Moules opening close to the little islands, taking on the form of a “river or canal”.
