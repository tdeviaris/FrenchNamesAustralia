Cap Tourville - Cape Tourville

Tourville’s career unfolded during a period of intense maritime rivalry between the European powers. Louis XIV’s France repeatedly fought the Dutch Republic, England and their allies in conflicts in which control of maritime routes and trade played an important role. Tourville notably commanded the French fleet at the Battle of Beachy Head/Béveziers in 1690, where it faced an Anglo-Dutch fleet. Two years later, during the operations at Barfleur and La Hougue, the French fleet again fought English and Dutch forces. These encounters belong to a shared European history in which alliances and adversaries shifted over the course of successive wars.

It is important, however, to emphasise that Tourville’s reputation did not rest solely on his military successes. He was also recognised as an experienced seaman and an organiser of large fleets. The signalling systems used under his command reflect the growing importance attached to coordinating ships and collective manoeuvres in late seventeenth-century navies (see illustration). For this reason, his name continued to occupy an important place in the professional culture of the French Navy long after his death.
