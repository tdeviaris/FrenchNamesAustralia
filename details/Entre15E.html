Port de l'Espérance - Port Esperance
Baie de l'Espérance - Esperance Bay

The two place names pay tribute to L'Espérance, the expedition's second vessel, and to the role she played during the voyage. Formerly the transport vessel La Durance, she was extensively refitted before departure and sailed under the command of Jean-Michel Huon de Kermadec alongside La Recherche. Giving her name to two anchorages several thousand kilometres apart was therefore more than the simple repetition of a place name: in each case, the name preserves the memory of a concrete contribution by the ship or her crew to the exploration and safety of the expedition.

In Tasmania, Port de l'Espérance recalls the reconnaissance carried out on 20 May 1792 by a boat from L'Espérance, commanded by de la Seinie, which revealed a particularly safe natural harbour, sheltered from the sea and supplied with fresh water. In Western Australia, Baie de l'Espérance received its name in different circumstances: after a particularly dangerous passage, L'Espérance was the first of the two frigates to enter the anchorage where they found a welcome refuge on 9 December 1792.

The two names therefore share the same origin, but they do not commemorate exactly the same episode. The first is linked to a reconnaissance carried out by men from L'Espérance; the second to the role of the vessel herself in finding a crucial shelter for both ships.

The charts also make it possible to follow the way in which this nomenclature was gradually constructed. Beautemps-Beaupré first worked on sketches intended to record routes, soundings, coastlines and anchorages. The names appearing on these documents, working sketches and fair chart drafts, were not necessarily definitive.

In the D'Entrecasteaux Channel, the future Port de l'Espérance thus appears, both on a working sketch and on the fair chart, under the functional designation “Port C”. In Western Australia, Beautemps-Beaupré wrote “port de l'Espérance” on one of his sketches and retained the same designation on the fair chart derived from it, whereas d'Entrecasteaux ultimately adopted the form “baie de l'Espérance” in his journal and in the published nomenclature.

This difference is revealing of the division of responsibilities within the expedition. The hydrographer surveyed and constructed the geographical representation; the commander had authority over the nomenclature ultimately adopted. A working sketch could therefore preserve a provisional name or a geographical designation different from the one later sanctioned by the command and carried onto the final chart.
